Revenue is climbing. Margin is not.

FINDING 02

Receivables are outrunning payables

Indexed to 2014, Accounts Receivable grew +81% while Accounts Payable actually shrank −5%. Total Assets grew 39% against revenue’s 11% — the balance sheet is expanding much faster than the business underneath it.

FINDING 03

Revenue concentrates. Inventory doesn’t follow it.

Software and Hardware make up 45% of the $121.3M product-line total. The warehouse doesn’t track that concentration — Robots is the clearest mismatch.

Robots holds 41% of all inventory value ($481K of $1.19M) but generates only 7.5% of revenue ($9.1M of $121.3M) — a likely overstock, sitting next to Blueprints at just 2.3% of revenue on the other end.

EXHIBIT B

Two years of orders, no real seasonality

4,976 orders span September 2017 through August 2019. Monthly revenue swings 2–3× with no repeating calendar pattern — real U.S. retail has a reliable November–December peak; this data doesn’t.

Despite the file’s name, Wholesale outweighs Retail 9-to-1. B2B ordering doesn’t follow consumer holiday cycles — which may explain the flat seasonality above.

EXCEPTIONS

Three things to fix before this gets reused

None of these break the numbers above, but they’re the kind of thing that erodes trust in a workbook over time.

01

Stale tab title in Overhead Costs.xlsx

The 2018 tab’s header still reads “Red30 Tech 2017 Overhead Costs” — carried over from the prior tab and never updated.

02

Inconsistent section header in Marketing Expenses.xlsx

The labor section is labeled “LABOR” on the 2017 tab and “MARKETING LABOR” on the 2018 tab, for the identical line items.

03

Split schema across the two order-level files

US Online Retail Sales.xlsx and US Sales by Region.xlsx describe the same 4,976 orders but never share a column: Discount and Order Total live in one, Employee and Region live in the other. Either file alone is incomplete.

CONCLUSION

What to trust, what to ignore

Treat the finance/ directory as two teaching datasets, not one financial model — and don’t reach for it as a market-growth reference.

Trust
  • The statement series (Balance Sheet → Overhead Costs) as one internally consistent five-year model
  • Product Line’s own revenue math — its two tabs agree to the penny
Treat separately
  • Any attempt to reconcile order-level or Product Line revenue against the income statement — they’re unrelated datasets
  • The order data as a seasonality example — it doesn’t carry a real holiday pattern
  • Red30 Tech’s growth rate as a market benchmark — it runs well under actual 2017–2019 retail and e-commerce growth
